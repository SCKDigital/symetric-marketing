Most tracking apps don't tell you what the first few weeks feel like, because the honest answer is: not much, at first. That's not a flaw to apologise for. It's how the whole thing works, and it's worth knowing up front so it doesn't feel like the app is broken when there's nothing to read yet.

## Week 1: you're just logging

Nothing to see. Nothing's wrong, symetric just needs real data before it can say anything useful about it. A few check-ins a day, thirty seconds each, inside a window you set. That's the whole job this week.

<b>If you miss a day, or three, it costs you nothing.</b> There's no streak to break and nothing resets. Pick it back up when you can, the gap itself is still information.

## Week 3: it knows your baseline

Once there's enough logged, symetric works out what normal actually looks like for you specifically, a rolling median of your own history, not a population average or a clinical cut-off. This is why the first few weeks matter: without your own baseline, symetric has nothing to compare a bad day against.

## Week 6: it starts recognising patterns

This is usually the first week there's something worth reading. A run of low days. A day everything dropped at once. A relationship between two domains, sleep and next-day energy, say. Every finding shown carries how much data it rests on, and if there isn't enough yet, symetric says so rather than guessing.

## By your next appointment: you have a record

Something that makes the subjective a little more objective. [Read a real sample report](/report) to see exactly what that looks like on paper.

## The honest bit

Six weeks is roughly how long it takes for a pattern to have real evidence behind it, not a promise that something specific will surface by then. Some weeks will have more to show than others, that's normal, and it isn't something you did wrong.
